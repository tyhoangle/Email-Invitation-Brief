1The core insight

Why this email exists — don't lose this in copy.

We are not selling software in this email. Internally, our goal is to find founding pilot partners — CaritaHub already runs this exact model in Singapore's Active Ageing Centres, and Vietnam is a brand-new category (day-care has just one reference site nationally — Long Biên, Hanoi, Jun 2026 — and ISHC clubs have no digital layer at all).

Important — internal goal only, not email copy
"We are recruiting 2–3 founding partners for 2026 pilots — one community programme, one residential operator, one hospital team" is our internal reasoning for why this campaign exists. It is not a sentence to put in the email. A cold mass email that opens with "we're recruiting founding partners" reads like a formal program announcement — too heavy for a first touch, and it front-loads an ask before the reader has any reason to trust us.

The email itself should stay as a warm, low-pressure invitation to visit and see the product. The partnership conversation — including the "2–3 founding partners" framing — belongs at the booth, in person, once there's a real conversation happening. Let the invitation open the door; let the founding-partner ask happen face to face.

The email's single job: get the reader to come to the booth and experience it live.

For this campaign, the people we most want to walk through that door are community programme contacts (ISHC club / VAE) and day-care operators.

5Email outline

Structure marketing should follow, in order.

Opening — the shared problem, not our product

1–2 lines. Ground it in something they already recognize: Vietnam's ageing pace, and that Decision 1116/1648 ask every commune for exactly this — a club, a volunteer team, a health record, day-care pilots — but there's no ready-made system yet for that layer.

The insight, segment-specific

2–3 lines, swapped per audience:

Day-care: "Vietnam has one reference day-care site today (Long Biên, Hanoi). We already run this exact model in Singapore's Active Ageing Centres. We want to bring it to a handful of Vietnamese day-care pilots, built together, not sold off a shelf."
ISHC/clubs: "Your club already runs on seven paper books and real trust. We're not asking you to change how it works — we want to make those books effortless, using the same architecture we run in Singapore."

The invite — soft, not a program pitch

1–2 lines. No "we're recruiting founding partners" sentence here — that's internal reasoning, not email copy. Instead, invite them to come see it and talk: "We'd love to show you how this works in person, and hear how it could fit what you're already doing." Warm and open, not a formal ask.

The invitation — event logistics

What: Vietnam Medi-Pharm Expo 2026 · When: 30 Jul – 1 Aug, 9:00–17:00 · Where: SECC, HCMC, Booth [xx]. What they'll see: a live demo — the club/day-care operations screen, the family app "photo moment," the printable books. Offer a specific next step: book a 15-min slot, or drop by anytime.

6Call to action

Pick ONE primary action per email — don't stack multiple asks.

Primary
"Reply to book a time to visit us at the booth" / "Scan to reserve a 15-minute slot at Booth [xx]"
Secondary (soft, for those who can't attend)
"Reply and we'll send you the site link and set up a call."

Never: "buy now," "sign up," or any transactional CTA — this is a relationship/invitation email, not a sales email.

7Hard guardrails

Non-negotiable — same discipline CaritaHub's own product uses internally (attention levels, never a diagnosis).

1

Lead every version with "data stays in Vietnam, on-premise" — never open with a feature list.

2

Never publish pricing. If cost comes up in replies: "pricing follows a pilot scoping visit."

3

Never say "medical device," "diagnosis," or "treatment." Say "attention level," never a clinical claim.

4

All Vietnamese copy needs native-speaker proofread before send — gate is 21 Jul, no exceptions.

5

Don't overstate club counts. HCMC's ISHC club number is genuinely unresolved (102 to 1,000+) — never quote a single figure externally.

6

Only use numbers from the Safe-to-Print Fact Sheet (Section 8) — anything else needs BD sign-off first.

7

Plain sentences — no idioms, no clever wordplay. Confident, not boastful.

8

Frame as invitation, not vendor pitch — keep the tone warm and open, not a formal program pitch.

9

Do not include the "we're recruiting 2–3 founding partners" sentence in the email itself. That's the internal reason we're doing this campaign — it belongs in the in-person booth conversation, not in the mass invite.
